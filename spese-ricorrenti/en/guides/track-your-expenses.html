How to track your expenses

Tracking your expenses doesn't take a spreadsheet or hours of your time. It takes a method simple enough to keep up every month. Here is the one that works, in six steps.

1. Separate fixed expenses from everyday ones

Fixed ones (rent, subscriptions, bills, insurance) repeat and can be predicted: you enter them once. Everyday ones (groceries, gas, coffee) need logging when they happen. Keeping them apart avoids counting anything twice.

2. Start with the fixed ones

They're the biggest and the easiest part: list them with amount and frequency and work out how much they weigh each month. That total already tells you how much is left.

3. Pick a few categories

Ten or twelve are enough. Too many categories make logging tiring and the result useless.

4. Log on the spot

The habit matters more than precision. The faster (or more automatic) logging is, the more likely you are still tracking a month from now.

5. Set a monthly budget

Decide how much you want to spend, or start from your salary, and now and then compare what has already gone out with what you have left.

6. Look at the trend once a month

You don't need to do it every day. A monthly check shows where the money really goes and which expenses you could cut.
