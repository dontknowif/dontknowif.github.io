How to track your subscriptions

One €9.99 subscription is no big deal. Ten of them add up to over a thousand euros a year, and you only remember many of them when the charge appears. Here is a five-step method to always know what you pay, when and why.

Updated 21 November 2026

1. List everything

Go through the last three months of bank statements, your receipt emails and, on iPhone, the subscriptions list in your Apple Account settings. Include the yearly ones: they are the easiest to forget.

2. Note four things for each

Name, amount, how often it renews and the date of the next charge. Add where to cancel too: when you decide to stop, you don't want to waste time looking for it.

3. Bring everything to the same period

A €120-a-year subscription costs €10 a month; one at €4.99 a month costs almost €60 a year. Converting everything into a monthly and a yearly total makes comparisons immediate.

4. Get warned before the charge

The right time to decide is before the renewal goes through, especially for yearly renewals and free trials that turn into paid ones. Set a reminder a few days before each due date.

5. Review every three or four months

Look for streaming services active at the same time (often one at a time, in rotation, is enough), price increases that went unnoticed, subscriptions you no longer use and duplicates.

The subscriptions people forget most often

Everyone remembers streaming and music. The ones that slip through are different, and they're usually the ones that cost the most over a year:

  • Yearly ones: gym, antivirus, insurance, memberships, sign-ups. They arrive once a year, when you no longer remember having them.
  • Free trials: started with your card, they turn into paid plans the day after they end, with no further warning.
  • In-app subscriptions: cloud storage, photo apps, meditation, games. They're billed through your Apple Account and don't show up under the service's name on your bank statement.
  • Bank and phone fees: account fees, cards, extra services from your carrier. Small fixed amounts nobody thinks of as subscriptions, but they are.
  • Family ones: the shared plan you pay for, your kids' app, the service only someone else at home uses.

How to spend less without giving everything up

Tracking is about choosing, not necessarily cutting. A few moves usually do it:

  • Rotate streaming: if you have three video services, you probably watch one at a time. Keeping one active for a few months and then switching costs a third, and the catalogues will still be there.
  • Yearly plans only for what you use all year: for services you'd never drop, yearly is usually cheaper. For everything else, monthly keeps you free.
  • Family plans: if two people at home pay for the same service, a shared plan costs less than two individual ones.
  • Write down when trials end: when you start a free trial, add it straight away as an expense with the date of the first charge and a reminder a few days before.
  • Watch for price rises: prices go up without anyone noticing. Comparing today's amount with last year's is the simplest way to decide whether a service is still worth what you pay.

How to cancel a subscription on iPhone

For subscriptions billed through Apple:

  1. Open Settings and tap your name at the top.
  2. Tap Subscriptions.
  3. Pick the subscription and tap Cancel Subscription.

You can usually keep using the service until the end of the period you've already paid for. Subscriptions paid directly to the provider, by card or direct debit, are cancelled on the provider's website or app instead, or as the contract says.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find all my active subscriptions on iPhone?

Open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions: that lists the ones billed through Apple. Subscriptions paid by card directly to the provider (many streaming services, gyms, insurance) don't appear there, so check your bank statements and receipt emails as well.

Is it better to pay a subscription monthly or yearly?

The yearly plan is usually cheaper for the same service, but only worth it if you use the service all year. For something you need for a few months, monthly lets you stop whenever you like. The simplest way to decide is to compare what both plans cost over a year.

If I pause or delete a subscription in Skurda, does it get cancelled?

No. Skurda tracks your expenses; it doesn't manage them. You cancel with the provider or in your Apple Account settings. That's why the savings report flags expenses paused in the app for more than 14 days: you may have stopped them only in Skurda.

How do I remember when a free trial ends?

As soon as you start it, add it as a recurring expense with the date of the first charge and a reminder a few days before. In Skurda, reminders before each due date are free, and you choose the notice period for each expense.

How often should I review my subscriptions?

Every three or four months is a good pace: often enough to catch price rises and services you no longer use, rarely enough that it doesn't become a chore. On top of that, look at each yearly subscription a few weeks before it renews.
